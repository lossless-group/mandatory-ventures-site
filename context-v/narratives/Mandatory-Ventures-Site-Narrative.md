# Mandatory Ventures: Site Narrative

> A narrative, not a spec. This is the story mandatory.ventures has to tell,
> agreed before anything is built. Grilled out with Michael on 2026-11-10 using
> `write-site-narrative`, starting from the decks in
> `Documents/Current Projects/Mandatory Ventures/` (2020 to 2025).

## Why Care?

Mandatory Ventures is an old brand for venture capital that Michael came up
with, with years of legacy material behind it. It's in formation again, and a
good place to park a few things going on. The site gives him something to point
to.

**The site's job: make large-scale capital, in one screen, want a conversation
about getting onto the cap tables of scaleups through the investors who are
already there.**

## Who Mandatory is (authoritative)

**Coordinating capital into the world's most important companies.**

Not an explicit fund-of-funds with a co-investment desk, as the 2021 decks
proposed. Something more flexible, holding:

- Deals brought to investors, when there are deals.
- Michael's venture-partner and adviser relationships.
- His thesis and writing.
- **A coalition, not a company:** a group of collaborators (no entity, no
  admin) where large-scale capital connects with early-stage investors who have
  access to the cap tables of companies that have gone from startup to scaleup.
  Its working name is never used in public; on the site it's simply "a
  coalition, not a company".

**From startup to scaleup** is important messaging. Large-scale capital doesn't
want to invest in startups, save to invest in the scaleups. Increasingly and
assertively, it wants access to "direct" cap-table investments in mid-stage
scaleups showing massive growth and universal brand recognition.

Mandatory is a play on mandates: when large-scale capital makes a strategic
decision to get exposure to something. **Matching mandates to earned access.**

## The two voices

### For large-scale capital (first)

Family offices, institutions, sovereign and strategic money. They have the
capital and a mandate for direct exposure to scaleups, and they can't get onto
the cap tables: the fastest-growing companies fill their rounds with insiders
and early backers. Mandatory is the way onto those cap tables through the
investors who are already on them. You partner with them; you don't just sit at
your desk in Midtown.

> Takeaway: *your mandate, matched to access someone earned.*

### For early-stage investors with scaleup access (second)

Angels, pre-seed and seed investors who saw it first, bet the real bet, and now
have unique access into scaleups. Bring your pro-rata and secondary allocations
to the capital that wants them.

> Takeaway: *the access you earned, matched to a mandate that needs it.*

## Why believe it

**People and trust are the inputs, deals are the outcome.** The site shows who
is involved and how they think. It never lists companies, allocations or
offerings: publicly marketing particular private investments can count as
general solicitation, and with large-scale capital, discretion is itself proof.

## Why this one

Every early-stage VC has followed Fifty Years in saying they only invest in the
most important companies and the world's best entrepreneurs. Saying that is now
undifferentiated. Meanwhile the world is awash with SPV Bros who just try to get
fees on deals. Mandatory's difference is earned access, coordinated.

## The feeling

**The look:** a 1960s corporate annual report that has sat on a desk or shelf
for decades. General Dynamics is the template. Off-white paper, typewriter
fonts, a high-contrast serif for headings, grain, red-pen marks in the margin.
The 2020 to 2021 decks already have most of it.

**The voice:** Monty Python-level intellectual humor, edgy on purpose. Most
large-scale capital teams are very smart and to some degree very bored at their
corporate, riskless jobs. A sense of humor might push away some or most, and
attract the right people.

**Where the satire stops:** the landing pages and first spike name no names;
they mock types (the SPV Bro, the desk in Midtown). The content collections
poke the bear and can name names. SoftBank is fair game all day.

**The meme:** Mandatory walks into a meme that already exists, the way Dropbox
did. Mandatory Fun is the cocktail hour.

## Lines in hand

- Hero: "Coordinating capital into the world's most important companies."
- Subhead: "Matching mandates to earned access."
- "From startup to scaleup."
- "People and trust are the inputs, deals are the outcome."
- From the decks: "Because it's mandatory." / "man•da•to•ry: so important it is
  required." / the curve: "We invest here." "So you look smart here." "This is
  when everyone starts pretending."

Not for the page: "Mandatory coordinates capital from those who have it to the
cap tables of companies that have made it." It's accurate, but it doesn't ring.

## What's on the site

In formation; the first version is something to point to. **It's a
destination, so it has no call to action**, just a way to connect on X and
LinkedIn.

1. **One home page:** hero, subhead, startup to scaleup with the curve redrawn,
   the two voices, people and trust, and links to connect on X and LinkedIn.
   No form, no database.
2. **Mandatory Reading** as a content collection from day one, even with one
   article, so the bear-poking has a home.
3. **A footer disclaimer:** "Not an offer. Mandatory Disclosures available on
   request."

Later, the site grows into a "Mandatory ___" vocabulary: Mandatory Fun
(gatherings in person), Mandatory Reading, Mandatory Meeting,
Mandatory Disclosures, Mandatory Terms.
